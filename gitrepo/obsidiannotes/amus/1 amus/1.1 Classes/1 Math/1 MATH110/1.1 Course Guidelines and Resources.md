# Course Guidelines and Resources

## Course Guidance

Read through this material completely before you begin.  
  

This course assumes the student has completed MATH101 Introduction to College Algebra or an equivalent course and is completely comfortable with the language of algebra, equations and inequalities, polynomials, factoring, and rational expressions.

**College Algebra**

If you are a little nervous, don't worry - math anxiety is common. However, if you review and practice what you learn in the LimeSpring materials, you should gain the confidence to succeed. Try your best to not put off assignments - practice and repetition increase your comfort level.  

If need a little extra help, there is plenty of help available.  Throughout MATH110 there are Handy Helpers and links to other, additional resources.  Tutoring is available.   And, this course in College Algebra is linked to its preceding course, Intermediate Algebra, through the "Foundational Knowledge" tab.  The course in Intermediate Algebra is part of the OpenStax textbooks maintained by Rice University at [https://openstax.org/ opens in new window](https://openstax.org/)  Just follow the links to topics you want to learn more about!  

Last, but definitely not least, if you have questions ask your instructor or contact your advisor. You've got this!  
  
**Syllabus vs. Lessons**  
 

The syllabus will give you the big picture on what we are studying and when. The lessons are structured to walk you through the details. Do NOT work from the syllabus—work from the lessons. They will direct you to resources and exercises to walk you through the topics of the course.  
 

**The Tests**

The tests are located in LimeSpring. Be sure to take the tests in a quiet location and allow sufficient time.  
  
**Disaster Preparedness**  
 

How not to mess up and what to do if you do!

- AVOID DISASTER by doing all the work. Even if this seems like review of math you have already seen, do not jump right in and take the test without working through the Lesson.
    

- HELP! If you still need to do more work to understand the math, Message me with your concerns. Together we can explore ways to bring you up to speed on the material.
    

- Be specific! Ask questions like "How do I determine which way a parabola opens." That will get you a targeted answer that addresses your specific concern. Saying, "_I just don't get any of this._" doesn't accomplish anything.
    

**If you are willing to work hard at mastering this material, I will be with you every step of the way.**

  
**A Word to the Wise**  
 

The math requirements for most degree programs were put there because people who are more experienced sincerely believe that the skills that you learn in this class will be helpful to you in the future. They may be as pertinent as calculating the slope to determine the pitch of a roof or as abstract as learning how to discern a pattern in one situation and apply it to another.

You are really not doing this work for me. You are doing this as an investment in your own future.

My role is to guide you along the way. My entire goal is to help you master this material without stress. If you have questions, ASK and I'll be happy to try and find resources to help you.

A email using my email address found under Classlist area in Course Tools is by far the fastest way to contact me.

  
**Getting answers to math questions**  
 

There are basically four ways to get answers to questions you have about the assigned work.

- Email me using my email address found under Classlist area in Course Tools. Hopefully, I can talk you through it, make a PowerPoint, or refer you to a website.
    

- Post a question in the Study Group Discussion. A fellow student might hop on with an answer even faster than I can. Do **not** ask for help with questions from the actual test!
    

- Find a real-life buddy who is good at math and ply them with treats (teenage children are especially handy for this purpose and they really enjoy the role reversal!)
    

- Use the resources provided ... There is a wealth of information in the Cheat Sheets and Handy Helpers. Explore the Helpers and try to find a site that explains things in a way that makes sense to you and then bookmark that baby. Different sites may be better for different topics, so if your first love goes south, start trolling for one of those other fish in the ocean!

## Program & Policy

While there are no prerequisites for MATH110, the course assumes the student has completed MATH101, Introduction to College Algebra, or an equivalent course and is completely comfortable with the language of algebra, equations and inequalities, polynomials, factoring, and rational expressions.  
  
Throughout the course, there is careful attention to the presentation of concepts that will become important in the study of analytic geometry, trigonometry and calculus.  
  
This mathematics course delivered via distance learning will enable students to complete academic work in a flexible manner, completely online. Course materials and access to an online learning management system will be made available to each student. Assigned faculty will support the students throughout this eight-week course.

The nature of an online course requires a significant amount of independent work. The student will be provided with structure, resources, guidance and instructor experience for learning the course material. The student, however, is responsible for managing time, completing assignments on time, completing the readings, and making inquiries as needed to complete the course effectively. Mathematics is best learned by devoting time to its study on a regular basis. This requires dedication and diligence on the part of the student.

There are weekly assignments and assessments (Homework, Discussions, and Unit Tests). All assignments must be submitted by the due date for the assignment.

## Discussions

All discussions can be accessed either through weekly Activities & Assessments or in the Discussions section of the course.  Students must make a substantive initial post to each of the weekly discussions. In addition, to get full credit, maximum points, most discussions require substantive replies to at least 2 other students. Substantive means that the post includes information about the topic of the week presented in an informative manner. Be sure to read the directions for each discussion before you try to complete them.  

Discussions exist for two reasons.  First, the discussions provide a mechanism for student-to-student and for student-to-faculty interaction.  Discussions, along with the message boards, are the primary mechanism for interaction in MATH110.  Second, discussions are due before virtually every other kind of coursework.  That way, discussions provide a way to find and address things before they become a real problem.  When you post a problem in a discussion, your classmates may be able to respond and help you with it.  If not, it may be something that your instructor needs to address.  Either way, whatever you post in your discussions will get attention. 

Students should demonstrate their own knowledge in the discussions. All Discussion posts should be written in grammatically correct English. If research is necessary to answer a question, then source(s) should be properly cited. If you have questions about how to cite work of others you are talking about or using in your own calculations you can learn more about citations at:

- The [Trefry Library opens in new window](https://www.apus.edu/apus-library/index.html)
- The Purdue University Online Writing Lab at [https://owl.purdue.edu/ opens in new window](https://owl.purdue.edu/) 

Be sure to join your classmates and earn all points possible in the discussions each week.  Let's all work together!

## Tests & Quizzes

Weekly tests are located in LimeSpring. These are open-book and open-note tests, but are not collaborative efforts.  All the work you submit on the Tests must be your own work.  If you have questions refer to your Student Handbook, or your Instructor.  

The final exam will be available in LimeSpring during the last week of the term and will be worth 20% of your final grade. Like the weekly tests, the Final Exam will be open-book and open-notes but you may not receive help from anyone.  You must submit only your own work. The comprehensive final exam will consist of all material covered during the entire class. You will not need a proctor to take the final exam.

The required math work for this course is the Homework, Weekly Tests, and the Discussions in the classroom. Students may get extra practice on the types of problems that we are learning to solve by using the LimeSpring workbook and many online sources.

## Citation and Reference Style

When citing an online reference for a Discussion be sure to give the exact URL of the website where you found the information.  This is a good way to start getting used to inserting citations to avoid the appearance of plagiarism.  And that way, others who are interested in the topic can go directly to your source as a starting point for additional reading.  If you ever have questions on citing sources or any other part of your writing requirements - just ask!  

In addition, there are many sources of help online, e.g. our own Trefrey Library at [https://www.apus.edu/apus-library/index.html opens in new window](https://www.apus.edu/apus-library/index.html) or the Purdue University's Online Writing Lab (OWL).  You will find the Purdue OWL at [https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/purdue_owl.html opens in new window](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/purdue_owl.html).

## Interaction Guidelines/Netiquette

Online universities promote the advancement of knowledge through positive and constructive debate -- both inside and outside the classroom. Discussions on the Internet, however, occasionally can degenerate into needless insults and “flaming.” Such activity and the loss of good manners are not acceptable in a university setting; basic academic rules of good behavior and proper “netiquette” must persist. Remember that you are in a place for the fun and excitement of learning that does not include descent to personal attacks, or attempts to intimidate or stifle the discussion of others. 

**Message Guidelines:** During the course of this class, all correspondence with me should take place as either a message via my email address in the class list or a Discussion post. Unless I am temporarily out of wi-fi range, I check messages daily and respond immediately.  
**Discussion Guidelines:** As you make your Discussion posts, keep in mind that in a college classroom the expectation is that you will communicate in full and complete sentences using correct spelling and grammar. I try to stop by the Discussions on a daily basis and give partial credit for the postings that have been made. You will receive a maximum of 6 points for your initial post and 2 points each for your responses to each of two classmates for a maximum of 10 points. Be sure to check the Grading Comment if you have received less than full credit. Those instructions should tell you what you need to do to remedy the situation.   
**Technology Limitations:** Avoid the use of attachments in Discussion posts since not all students have the necessary software to open attachments.
